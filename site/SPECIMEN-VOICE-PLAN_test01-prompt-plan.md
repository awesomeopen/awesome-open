# Test recipe:
- 2 takes of each project's A/B/C, plus 2x2 neutral takes of each: 30 clips total.
- Keep generation settings fixed. Record seeds if your interface exposes them; otherwise label the takes 01 and 02.
- Save raw WAVs, for example openscad-character-name-01.wav and openscad-character-carrier-01.wav. Don’t trim yet.
- Note clipped beginnings/endings, wrong pronunciation, extra sounds, and whether the strange voice survives the short name. Decide your intended acronym readings before scoring pronunciation.
- Carrier phrase: "The product is called [NAME]."

# Tests:

## 1. OpenSCAD — a measuring creature
Voice description: A tiny mechanical creature speaking through a narrow ceramic mouthpiece. High, dry, nasal timbre, precisely clipped consonants and evenly measured syllables. Almost flat intonation, deliberate pacing, with each word placed as carefully as a geometric component. Neutral English accent. Every syllable is complete and intelligible.
Spoken text A: OpenSCAD.
Spoken text B: The product is called OpenSCAD.
Spoken text C: OpenSCAD creates solid three-dimensional models from scripts.

Strange resonance, but clear words without added background sounds.
Spoken text A: OpenVPN.

Its speech has the tense precision of a mechanism issuing tactical instructions. Crisp English pronunciation, energetic without shouting, with all words fully spoken.
Spoken text A: OpenRA.
Spoken text B: The product is called OpenRA.

## 4. OpenCat — an articulated walking thing
Voice description: A curious four-legged mechanical creature with a light, reedy, slightly creaky voice. Springy rhythm, small upward bends in pitch and rounded vowels. Its delivery seems to balance carefully from syllable to syllable. Inquisitive rather than cute, with a neutral English accent and clear consonants. Speech only, without meowing or other animal sounds.
Spoken text A: OpenCat.
Spoken text B: The product is called OpenCat.
Spoken text C: OpenCat controls the coordinated movements of four-legged robots.
