# Style atlas — macro

Use this file for visual direction, source provenance, and examples. Treat the interpretations as a design synthesis, not claims that these sources share one historical movement. The new style's distinction is **discoverable function inside unfamiliar form, drawn with calm observational precision and selective graphic intensity**.

This atlas is adapted from the original Speculative FOSSoligy reference record. The access ledger below preserves observations recorded on 2026-10-12; it records inherited research. Macro extends the drawing grammar to people, inhabited spaces, and landscapes.

## Translate the influences

| Reference family | Extract | Transform for Speculative FOSSoligy | Leave behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imaginary encyclopedia plates | Isolated subjects, exact-looking contours, serial variation, shaded color, impossible transformations | Give the subject an intelligible local operation; make a single drawing work without surrounding manuscript apparatus | Copied inventions, obligatory unreadable writing, simulated book spreads |
| Speculative natural history | Adaptation, posture, load-bearing structure, continuity of anatomy | Make each oddity affect a behavior; extend that reasoning to tools and shelters | Claims of scientific prediction; requirement for realistic evolution |
| Psychedelic print illustration | Strong chromatic boundaries, repeated organic rhythms, flat regions beside stippled depth | Concentrate intensity within the subject's active structures | Copied poster compositions, obligatory typography, all-over pattern |
| Early fantastical creature painting | Matter-of-fact animal behavior, disquieting but connected hybrid anatomy | Join unfamiliar parts through continuous tissue or structure | Borrowed religious narratives, copied tableaux, compulsory moral symbolism |

Do not average all influences equally in every drawing. Retain observational linework and connected construction as the core; vary chromatic boldness, density, and biological/mechanical emphasis by subject. A restrained blue-and-ochre tool and a vividly patterned seed organism should still belong together.

## Original concept examples

Use these to understand the grammar; derive each new scene from its target.

- **Conversation tool:** Two people tune flexible listening canopies in a shared courtyard; a woven span tightens where their exchanges find common rhythm. Show gestures, seating, and the span's attachments.
- **Infrastructure tool:** Residents and maintainers rearrange reusable ceramic service bays beneath inhabited rooms. Show one repair and how the shared structure supports daily life.
- **Historical register:** Archivists and visitors follow successive repairs through an inhabited civic building whose structural layers unfold into accessible records. Keep the path and one act of comparison legible.
- **Transport project:** Ground crews load a suspended airship above a settlement; articulated mooring ribs reveal how wind and cargo affect the departure route. Let the landscape explain the journey.
- **Consumption:** People prepare and share food around a table whose folded channels redistribute heat between dishes. Concentrate color at the working surfaces.
- **Unoccupied dwelling:** A rain-sharing house shows worn handles, repaired gutter joints, drying clothes, and cups set beneath an unfolding cistern. Make the household's routines visible.
- **Faithful redraw:** Preserve the people, street, poses, perspective, and building geometry of a supplied scene; translate it into fine contours, selective pencil grain, and focused color.

## Drift corrections

- **Generic fantasy:** Replace magical decoration with a specific behavior and visible structural consequence.
- **Random collage:** Reduce to one governing transformation; connect material and joints.
- **Direct pastiche:** Remove copied motifs, manuscript furniture, and recognizable compositions; invent from function.
- **Overly tame:** For invention, change a structural relationship rather than merely applying odd colors. For faithful edits, respect fidelity and intensify only permitted rendering choices.
- **Overcrowded:** Simplify secondary appendages and reserve fine work for explanatory areas.
- **Too polished:** Replace glossy highlights and broad gradients with controlled marks, flatter color, and quiet paper ground.
- **Too vintage:** Remove stains, yellowing, deckled edges, and artificial distress; keep drawing character.
- **Too cute or menacing:** Use observed expressions and restrained light; show an ordinary action calmly.

- **Human presence feels incidental:** Rebuild the focal action around what people do or what their use has left behind. Connect the project to that action.
- **Scenic clutter:** Keep one main relationship and subordinate distant activity through simpler contours and quieter color.
